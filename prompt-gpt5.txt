You are a senior database designer. Design a conceptual ER diagram for a new dental practice and deliver it as a Mermaid erDiagram.

BUSINESS DESCRIPTION

The owners of a new, not-yet-launched dental practice have hired you to design a database that will help run their operation. They want the big pieces in place.

1. FINANCING. They are taking a $300,000 loan from a local bank, to be paid off with interest in installments spanning 10 years. Startup costs include furniture, dental equipment, software (scheduling, billing), supplies, and training.

2. STAFF. They will hire front office workers, dental hygienists, dentists, periodontists, endodontists, orthodontists, and dental surgeons. The medical professionals (not the front office staff) hold state licenses that must be kept up to date, and the business needs to track licensure status. Everyone, including the doctors, is paid a monthly salary. There are no hourly rates and no overtime.

3. PATIENTS. They expect to service around 100 patients. Each patient's billing record consists of their insurance information (insurance provider name, insurance subscriber ID, coverage type such as "savings plan", amount of insurance coverage) and payment details (amount owed, amount paid by insurance, amount paid by the patient). Each patient's medical record consists of procedures (e.g. teeth cleaning), treatments (e.g. gum disease) and surgeries (e.g. tooth extraction).

4. INSURANCE. Most patients carry dental insurance, so the staff maintains a list of insurance providers. Billing an insurance provider consists of submitting the patient's insurance subscriber ID, the type of treatment (its CDT code, e.g. D1110), the treatment date, the amount requested/billed/claimed, and the amount paid by the insurance company to the practice.

5. DAILY OPERATIONS. Hygienists and doctors (together called "doctors") serve patients in one of 8 rooms, which are scheduled for a specific doctor and patient. Front office staff routinely contact patients to do the scheduling. A patient, at any time, is in exactly one of these scheduling states: contacted, scheduled, recently visited, up for next visit, dormant.

6. FACILITIES AND RECURRING COSTS. They will lease a medical building near a major hospital, following a monthly lease schedule. Supplies are restocked monthly, everything from needles to drugs to paper towels. There are other monthly operating costs: facilities cleaning, utilities, food in the breakout room.

7. REPORTING. Each morning the owners want to see the day's schedule. At the end of each day they want a report of the billable income generated by that day's services. Each month they want a report of expenditure and income so they can assess profit or loss. Longer term they want to take a data-driven analytics approach to maximizing ROI. Your model must make these reports derivable from stored data rather than storing the reports themselves.

DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

- Choose the entities yourself. Cover all seven areas above.
- Every entity must participate in at least one relationship. Orphan entities are the single most common failure here, so check your diagram for them before you answer.
- Get cardinality directions right. A lookup entity is on the "one" side of its relationship. A subtype is 1:1 with its supertype, not 1:many.
- Model the medical professional versus front office distinction as a specialization, and attach licensure tracking only to medical professionals.
- Resolve every many-to-many relationship with an associative entity.
- Give every entity a primary key marked PK, and mark foreign keys FK. Do not mark the same entity's PK twice, and do not put FK markers on dates or descriptive fields.
- Make reasonable assumptions about anything the description leaves unstated, and say what they were.
- Feel free to produce an EER-style design (specialization/generalization) if it fits, expressing subtypes as 1:1 relationships in Mermaid.

OUTPUT FORMAT

First, output one code block that opens with three backticks followed by the word mermaid. The FIRST LINE INSIDE that block must be the word erDiagram on its own line. Do not put erDiagram in the code fence tag itself; it must be a line inside the block.

After the code block, output a line with three dashes, then a section titled "Design decisions" as a bulleted list explaining why the design looks the way it does: which entities you merged or split and why, what assumptions you made, and anything from the description you deliberately did not model. Be specific rather than generic.

MERMAID SYNTAX NOTES

- Attribute lines are "type name" with an optional PK, FK or UK marker last: int patient_id PK
- Types must be plain words: string, int, decimal, date, datetime, boolean. No varchar(50), no numeric(10,2), nothing with parentheses or commas.
- Entity and attribute names use only letters, digits and underscores.
- Relationship lines look like: PATIENT ||--o{ APPOINTMENT : books
- A relationship label must be a single word, or wrapped in double quotes if it contains spaces.
